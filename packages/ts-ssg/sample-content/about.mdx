## Why this example exists

The starter gives you a connected set of pages instead of isolated component demos. A visitor can move from the [home page](/) to the [platform story](/platform/), explore a [workspace preview](/workspace/), compare [plans](/plans/), and read a [journal article](/journal/weekly-review/). Each page serves a recognizable purpose.

Waypoint's product details and numbers are illustrative. They are content examples, not a service you can sign up for. The patterns underneath are the useful part: reusable sections, strong navigation, responsive layouts, accessible components, and a guide that explains where to change things.

## Make it yours

Start with the audience and the promise. Then replace the brand, sample data, plan details, and links. Connect any form or account flow to your own backend before launch. The [getting started guide](/guide/getting-started/) walks through those steps in order.

<AlertBox title="A useful starting rule" tone="info">
  Keep the parts that explain your product. Remove the parts that do not. A strong starter should make editing easier, not dictate your final site map.
</AlertBox>
